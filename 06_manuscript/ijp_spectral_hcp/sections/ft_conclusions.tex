\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions_dynamic}

This work asks which perturbation is selected by the complete loading history of an evolving HCP crystal, rather than which frozen eigenvalue is largest at one state. The main conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The 84-coordinate quadratic crystal descriptor admits exact elimination of 18 algebraic micromorphic coordinates. An independent 87-coordinate QZ audit recovers 69 finite and 18 structural infinite roots and matches the reduced 69-state generator to within $8.60\times10^{-8}$ in relative root error, without fictitious microfield inertia.
\item Accumulating frozen spectral abscissae is not equivalent to finite-time propagation. Stationary-normal and commuting-normal fixed-leader controls recover equality to $10^{-14}$. On the complete 69-state space with identity selectors, the onset-$x$, onset-$y$ and terminal-$y$ branches instead give $\log\mathcal G^{\rm full}-\int\alpha\,\mathrm dt=+4.784$, $+7.217$ and $+6.402$. The separately reported projected gain is not mixed with this full-state comparison.
\item Large modal condition numbers, rotating leading eigenvectors and noncommuting adjacent generators explain the discrepancy. Reversing terminal chronological factors lowers the log gain from 13.370 to 10.979, and a first-Magnus surrogate gives 10.115. Three independently selected Bunge orientations retain positive like-for-like discrepancies, so the effect is not a single-orientation exception.
\item The selector is horizon and input--output dependent but robust within each declared alternative. Across all 11 factor-two norms, five of seven near-onset selector questions choose onset-$y$ and two choose terminal-$y$; at $140~\mu\mathrm s$, six choose terminal-$y$ and mechanical/mechanical chooses onset-$x$. The baseline threshold is crossed at approximately $1.46~\mu\mathrm s$, with $x$- and $y$-like branches within 2\%. By $140~\mu\mathrm s$, its separated tested optimum is $y$-like with $k=7.724\times10^3~\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and gain $6.39\times10^5$.
\item Nested 64/256/1024-point reconnaissance, exact anchors, eight independent refinements per horizon and 129-state verification retain the selected basins on the registered domain. Because blind 1024-point Sobol sampling misses the narrow terminal basin, this evidence is an anchor-assisted convergence audit rather than an independent global-optimum certificate.
\item The coupling architecture reorganizes with time. Exact 64-coalition attribution changes its leading edge from dislocation--plastic near onset to inertia--plastic at the terminal horizon, showing that dominant state amplitude and dominant coupling dependence are distinct concepts.
\item Full-rank slip-gradient recovery suppresses the sampled high-$k$ quasi-static branch more completely than the rank-deficient Nye contribution alone. This supports finite-band regularization on the audited symbol but does not establish a nonlinear material band thickness.
\item The complete nonlinear periodic residual reproduces the actual optimized near-onset and terminal singular-vector gains within 1.24\% and 0.223\%, with complete complex output-vector errors below 1.41\% and factor-two amplitude collapse. A separate same-window width audit nevertheless rejects seed-independent FWHM, 12-cell multimode resolution and an emergent material width. The theory closes finite-time linear selection and its small-amplitude nonlinear transport; finite-width and propagation-resistance closure remain open.
\item The power/heat bookkeeping satisfies a first-law and entropy-production statement, while the nonthermal remainder is explicitly passive rather than asserted to be dislocation free energy. A 74-row parameter--source table and publication archive release the generators, complete singular vectors, norm--selector matrix, mechanism histories, anchor-assisted search trajectories, nonlinear validation fields and locked runtime environment. A strict HCP-to-Bai analytical degeneration remains an explicit non-required open gate.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting contribution is therefore sharply defined: existing frozen theories cannot determine which crystal-resolved disturbance wins after an evolving multi-physics history, whereas the time-ordered HCP propagator can. The same evidence also prevents a stronger but unsupported claim that the selected linear wavelength is already a material shear-band width.
