\title{Finite-time direction--wavenumber selection in evolving HCP crystal plasticity: exact descriptor reduction and path-dependent amplification}

\author{[Author names and affiliations to be supplied]}

\begin{abstract}
Frozen adiabatic-shear analyses rank instantaneous roots but do not identify
which crystal-resolved disturbance is amplified most along an evolving path.
We formulate this finite-time selection problem for an 18-slip HCP crystal.
Fourier linearization gives an 84-coordinate differential--algebraic
descriptor; exact elimination of 18 micromorphic fields yields a nonsingular
69-state generator.  A time-ordered propagator selects direction and
wavenumber for seven input--output maps.  Stationary and commuting-normal
controls recover frozen-spectrum equality under its sufficient conditions;
HCP paths depart because of non-normality, eigenvector rotation and chronology.
Seventy selector--norm--horizon optimizations separate high-frequency elastic
boundary branches from interior constitutive-response maxima; the baseline
terminal constitutive map selects $k=6.84\times10^3~\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and gain
$6.29\times10^5$.  The basin transfers across three added loading paths and
four local parameter perturbations.  Dealiased 16/32/64-cell replays converge
in gain, with the 32-to-64-cell change below $1.4\times10^{-7}$; independent
time-step and integration-format ladders also agree.  However, the primary
terminal audit places 84.7\% of the instantaneous right-hand-side energy
above the retained cutoff, so nonlinear spectral closure remains open.  The
titanium-like card is therefore a verification seed.  The framework closes
selector-dependent finite-time linear selection, not material band width,
calibrated dissipation or propagation resistance.
\end{abstract}
