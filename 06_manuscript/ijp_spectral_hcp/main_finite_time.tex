\title{Finite-time perturbation selection in evolving HCP crystal plasticity: path-dependent direction, wavelength and mechanism}

\author{[Author names and affiliations to be supplied]}

\begin{abstract}
Frozen adiabatic-shear theories rank instantaneous roots but do not determine
which crystal-resolved disturbance accumulates the largest amplification while
slip activity, dislocation populations and temperature evolve. We formulate
that finite-time selection problem for an 18-slip HCP crystal. Fourier
linearization gives an 84-coordinate quadratic differential--algebraic
descriptor; exact elimination of 18 algebraic micromorphic fields yields a
nonsingular 69-state generator. An independent QZ audit recovers 69 finite and
18 structural infinite roots, with a maximum finite-root mismatch of
$8.60\times10^{-8}$. A fixed-norm, time-ordered propagator then selects
direction, wavenumber and state composition. On the complete 69-state space,
stationary-normal and commuting-normal controls recover
$\log\|\Phi\|_2=\int\alpha\,\mathrm dt$ to $10^{-14}$ when a common leading
mode exists. The corresponding HCP differences are $+4.78$, $+7.22$ and
$+6.40$ for three audited branches, and remain $+3.31$--$+6.74$ at the
terminal horizon for three independently selected orientations. Large modal
condition numbers, eigenvector rotation and reverse-order propagation
quantify the non-normal and chronological origins of the discrepancy.
Eleven norm definitions and seven input--output selectors retain the
full/full and constitutive-localization $y$-like winners while exposing the
few selector questions that choose a different branch. An
anchor-assisted sphere--wavenumber audit retains these basins, while a blind
1024-point Sobol design misses the narrow terminal basin and therefore does
not constitute a global-optimum certificate. The complete nonlinear periodic
residual reproduces the near-onset and terminal singular-vector gains within
1.24\% and 0.223\%, respectively. The theory closes finite-time perturbation
selection, but neither identifies a seed-independent mature band width nor
provides propagation-resistance closure.
\end{abstract}
