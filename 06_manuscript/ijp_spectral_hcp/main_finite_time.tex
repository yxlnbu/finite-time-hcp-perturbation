\title{Finite-time perturbation selection in evolving HCP crystal plasticity: path-dependent direction, wavelength and mechanism}

\author{[Author names and affiliations to be supplied]}

\begin{abstract}
Frozen adiabatic-shear analyses rank instantaneous roots but cannot identify
which crystal-resolved disturbance wins along an evolving loading path.  We
pose this finite-time selection problem for an 18-slip HCP crystal.  Fourier
linearization gives an 84-coordinate differential--algebraic descriptor;
exact elimination of 18 micromorphic fields yields a nonsingular 69-state
generator.  A time-ordered propagator selects direction and wavenumber for
seven input--output maps.  Stationary and commuting-normal controls recover
frozen-spectrum equality, whereas HCP paths depart through non-normality,
eigenvector rotation and chronology.  Re-optimization under five norms
separates compact-domain elastic waves from interior constitutive-localization
maxima; the terminal constitutive map selects
$k=6.84\times10^3~\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and converged gain
$6.29\times10^5$.  Same-selector piecewise-frozen products converge to the
ordered map.  Three added rate/temperature paths and four local parameter
perturbations preserve the terminal basin but expose scale sensitivity.  A
four-mode Galerkin replay reproduces both optimized vectors at small
amplitude, but full-Fourier nonlinear continuation becomes non-finite.
Synchronized Grade-II titanium data retain only qualitative
stress-before-temperature ordering, not timing or magnitude.  The framework
therefore closes finite-time linear selection; mature width, calibration and
propagation resistance remain open.
\end{abstract}
